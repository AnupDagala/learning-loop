# Research plan — not completed research

No interviews, usability sessions or field observations have been conducted for this prototype.

## Discovery

Recruit 5–8 adult competitive-exam learners with varied preparation stages, including people who abandoned a learning tool. Ask about a recent real practice session before showing the prototype. Obtain consent and avoid storing names, contact details or sensitive exam history in the event pipeline.

Questions:

1. Walk me through the last question you got wrong. What happened next?
2. How did you decide what to revisit? Show the artifact or workflow if comfortable.
3. When did you next practice that topic? What prompted you?
4. When has feedback felt unhelpful? What specifically was missing?
5. What competes with returning: time, confidence, content quality, motivation, or something else?

Look for concrete episodes and disconfirming evidence. Do not ask whether they would use our feature as the main proof of demand.

## Usability tasks

Show a lesson without explaining the interface. Ask the participant to practice, interpret one mistake, choose a next action, and find the saved plan on a later visit. Record task completion, hesitation, expectation mismatches and what they believe was saved. Ask them to explain the constitutional/economic distinction in their own words.

## Decision gates

- If learners already have a strong revision workflow, investigate integration rather than replacing it.
- If the difficulty is the explanation itself, improve question/explanation quality first.
- If saving is easy but practice does not happen, revisit the metric and return mechanism.
- If repeated practice improves recognition but not transfer, use new equivalent questions before making learning claims.
